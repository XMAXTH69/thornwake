SHIP COCKPIT RULE

Glintline ships rework · 25 Nov 2026 · checked automatically in every ship build (ship_c_lib canopy_check)

“The cockpit must not be a too noticeable bump.”
“The metal on top of the cockpit is too wide, it covers too much.” (KB7 V11)

THE RULE - 3 NUMBERS

BUMP
≤ 20 %
How far the glass rises above the spine line (a straight line from the hull top in front of the glass to the hull top behind it), measured against the body depth there. The canopy rides in the body line, like the SR-71 or YF-23 - not a dome sitting on top.
METAL OVER THE GLASS
≤ 5 %
Bows and hoops across the glass, as a share of the canopy length. Thin bows only (about 2 cm), with tight glass rings each side so the smoothing cannot spread them wide.
FRONT STEP
≤ 4 cm
The glass starts flush with the hull at the front: no collar, no step at the windscreen.

REFERENCES - GLASS IN THE BODY LINE

YF-23 · canopy into the spine - the glass flows back into a long hump; no step, no collar.NASA/DFRC, public domain · source
SR-71 · flush canopy - the canopy is part of the chine line; you barely see it from the side.USAF/NASA, public domain · source
F-22 · no frame at all - one-piece glass, no bow across it: the most glass, the least metal.USAF, public domain · source
F-35 · one thin bow - a single narrow frame between front and rear glass = the KB7 V12 idea.JSF program, public domain · source
B-2 · cockpit inside the wing - the flying-wing extreme: the windows sit in the body hump, nothing sticks up.USAF, public domain · source
